## How the two approaches differ

### Static drills
Drill-based apps give you a fixed set of exercises written in advance: fill in the case ending, pick the right article, reorder the words. This works well for building habits on rules you already know you need, and it is easy to track progress.

The limit is that the exercises are not your sentences. When you hit a sentence in a book, a message or a work email that doesn't match a drill, you have nothing to check it against.

### Live explanatory breakdown (Epsylo)
Epsylo starts from the sentence you actually have. Type or paste it, and Epsylo translates it and shows, for each segment:

- its grammatical role (colour-coded),
- its dictionary form,
- an explanation of why it has that form in this sentence,
- alternatives you could use instead.

Syntax cards and dependency trees show how the words connect. You can then change politeness (four levels) or emphasis, down to a single word, and watch the sentence and its breakdown change.
